# Figure Legends

## Figure 1 | Shared transcriptional abnormalities and recurrence across brain regions

**A**, AD and control donor–tissue records across six regions. Technical repeats were collapsed. The inventory contains 20 GEO series, 28 cohort–region contributions and 17 conservative source families; 2,116 expression profiles correspond to 2,041 donor–tissue records, not unique people. **B**, Independently embedded, outcome-blind t-SNE maps for three multiregion cohorts. Color indicates region; filled/open symbols denote AD/control. Coordinates are not shared across cohorts. **C**, Whole-brain random-effects Meta coefficients and BH FDR. Significant negative and positive estimates are purple and red; other estimates are grey. No additional coefficient threshold defines significance. Estimates beyond ±1.5 or −log10(FDR)>20 are boundary-marked and retained in the tables. SNAP25, SYP and CCL2 are labelled for orientation. **D**, Signed Meta evidence versus the saved direction-consistent SumRank strength for jointly tested genes; Spearman correlation is shown. **E**, Genes significant at BH FDR<0.05 in at least one to six regions, among 19,650 jointly tested genes. The y-axis is logarithmic; recurrence does not require concordant signs. **F–G**, GO BP and KEGG over-representation among the 1,327 genes significant in at least three regions. Bar length denotes overlap count; color denotes BH FDR. Eight significant terms per database are displayed in FDR order; the full tested collections, including zero overlaps, enter correction. Each database uses its corresponding annotated common background. CB, cerebellum; OCC, occipital/visual cortex; F, frontal/prefrontal cortex; T, temporal neocortex; HIP, hippocampus; EC, entorhinal cortex.

## Figure 2 | Regional mechanism patterns and changes after stratification

**A**, Classical Hedges’ g for 100 genes across 28 cohort–region units and six regional random-effects meta-analyses. Of 528 genes significant in at least four regions (BH FDR<0.05), the 100 with the largest mean absolute meta-analytic g across all six regions are shown. Rows are clustered by average linkage on within-cohort percentile ranks; cohort columns retain their inventory order. Outlined, wider columns show regional estimates and do not enter clustering. Red and blue-purple denote higher and lower expression in AD, respectively (scale clipped at ±2); grey denotes missing data. Dots indicate BH FDR<0.05, corrected over all tested genes in the corresponding unit or region. **B**, Six fixed biological domains tested using cameraPR with assumed inter-gene correlation 0.01. Color denotes direction multiplied by −log10(FDR); stars denote BH FDR<0.05 within each regional six-domain analysis. **C**, Representative GO biological processes mapped directly to six TREAT-AD domains. Terms were ranked by the number of regions with GO-wide BH FDR<0.05, followed by the median absolute regional Z statistic, then GO ID. Redundant terms were removed by membership overlap; each domain contributed at most six terms, including shared terms. The continuous strip beside the row labels marks domain membership; a two-domain term has equal left and right color segments. Color shows the signed regional Z statistic; dots indicate GO-wide BH FDR<0.05. **D–E**, Marginal medians of absolute model-derived g* and I² on the matched regional–whole-brain backgrounds. Each line connects two summaries for the same region. These are not medians of the paired per-gene differences. Model-derived g* uses saved model coefficients, standard errors and group sizes rather than a direct raw-group standardized mean difference. Full data retain every region and both effect directions.

## Figure 3 | Frontal bulk patterns in astrocytes across transcriptomic sources

**A**, Frontal bulk-pattern correspondence across eight cell classes for six domain-level templates. All 2,737 distinct BP terms directly annotated to these domains in TREAT-AD were included, with genes deduplicated within each domain. Templates retained frontal bulk-significant members and their effect signs; donor-level local CPS associations were tested with directional fry. Dots denote BH FDR<0.05 across all 48 combinations. Red indicates correspondence, not pathway activation. **B**, All frontal-significant terms from Fig. 2C (35 of 35), in matching row order. Each bulk template comprises frontal-significant gene members weighted by their bulk effect sign. In each of eight SEA-AD frontal cell classes, a directional fry test examined association with donor-level local pathology (CPS). Red denotes correspondence and purple opposition to the template; intensity represents signed −log10(P). Dots denote BH FDR<0.05 across 280 planned comparisons. Grey indicates fewer than ten measurable template genes. The outline marks astrocytes. The row annotation strip uses the same order and six domain colors as Fig. 2C, including left/right halves for dual membership. **C**, Genome-wide significant genes in frontal bulk Meta, SEA-AD frontal astrocyte local-CPS associations and ROSMAP-427 prefrontal astrocyte gpath associations, restricted to 13,416 jointly testable genes. Each source uses its original genome-wide FDR<0.05. Seventy-two genes are significant in all three, including 67 with concordant signs and five with mixed signs. Circle area is not proportional to set size. **D**, All 67 concordant genes, displayed in one continuous heatmap on the right, ordered by descending unrounded Mean (ties alphabetically), with 56 positive and 11 negative associations. Genes were not filtered by a GO term, mechanism or effect-size rank. The first three columns show original coefficients: the bulk AD–control coefficient, SEA-AD local-CPS coefficient per original-model standard deviation, and the authors’ ROSMAP gpath coefficient. Mean is their unweighted arithmetic average, displayed only as a descriptive numeric summary. Their units differ, so Mean is not a pooled effect estimate and is not used for significance, mechanism-strength comparisons or gene prioritization; no additional P value or FDR is assigned to it. Background colors divide each value by its column-specific maximum absolute value among these 67 genes. SEA-AD uses the saved unadjusted donor-level pseudobulk model. Template correspondence tests a bulk-selected pattern rather than a complete-GO response. Concordance is a gene-selection criterion.

## Supplementary Figure 1 | Sample structure in all 20 GEO cohorts

Each cohort was embedded independently using the frozen outcome-blind procedure and saved coordinates. Color and symbol fill follow Figure 1. Donor–tissue records are not assumed to be independent people across brain regions. Parameters, variable-gene lists and coordinates remain available in the upstream record.
